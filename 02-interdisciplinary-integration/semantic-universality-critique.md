## The Adaptation Framework

**The Core Question**: How do we adapt meaning across cultures without losing the original intent?

**The Approach**: Semantic media compression relies on identifying a **gradient of universality**—distinguishing between elements of human experience that are shared and those that are culturally specific.

---

## The Gradient of Universality

To make adaptation work, we classify content elements into three tiers:

### Tier 1: Highly Universal (Direct Translation)
These elements can be extracted and moved across cultures with minimal change.
- **Basic Emotions**: Fear, joy, sadness, anger, surprise.
- **Physical Causality**: Actions have consequences (dropping a glass breaks it).
- **Biological Realities**: Hunger, fatigue, aging, family bonds.
- **Narrative Structure**: Problem → Struggle → Resolution.

### Tier 2: Culturally Malleable (Transformation Required)
These elements exist in most cultures but take very different forms. They require **semantic transformation**.
- **Social Etiquette**: How to show respect, how to greet, how to eat.
- **Expression of Love**: Verbal vs. non-verbal, public vs. private.
- **Humor**: Wordplay (untranslatable) vs. situational irony (adaptable).
- **Conflict Resolution**: Direct confrontation vs. subtle negotiation.

### Tier 3: Deeply Specific (Preservation or Annotation)
These are unique to a culture and should often be preserved as "foreign" elements or explained, rather than adapted, to avoid erasing cultural identity.
- **Sacred Rituals**: Specific religious or spiritual ceremonies.
- **Historical Events**: Facts that cannot be changed.
- **Specific Geographic Context**: A story set in Paris should probably still look like Paris.

## The "Romantic Love" Example

**Challenge**: Adapting a Western romantic comedy for a culture with different courtship norms (e.g., traditional arranged marriage contexts).

**Western Original**:
- **Action**: Couple kisses in public to show reconciliation.
- **Semantic Meaning**: "They have overcome their conflict and are reunited."

**Cultural Adaptation (Semantic approaches)**:
- **If Direct Translation**: The public kiss might be seen as shameful or offensive, ruining the emotional moment.
- **If Semantic Adaptation**: The system identifies the meaning ("Reunion/Reconciliation") and generates the appropriate cultural expression:
    - *Japan*: A deep bow or subtle exchange of meaningful glances.
    - *Conservative Society*: A respectful distance but warm verbal exchange.
    - *Western*: The original public kiss.

**Result**: The *audience feels the same emotion* (relief, joy), even though the *action* on screen is different.

---

## Practical Methodology

1.  **Extraction**: AI identifies the core narrative beats and emotional arcs (The "Why").
2.  **Classification**: Elements are tagged as Universal, Malleable, or Specific.
3.  **Adaptation**: 
    - Universal elements are rendered directly.
    - Malleable elements are passed through a "Cultural Filter" (e.g., "Apply Japanese Politeness Standards").
4.  **Generation**: The scene is recreated with the new cultural parameters.

## Conclusion

We do not claim that all meaning is universal. Instead, we build a system that respects cultural differences by adapting the *expression* of meaning to fit the *context* of the audience, ensuring the story's heart remains intact.
